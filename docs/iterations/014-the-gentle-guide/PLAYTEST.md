# Mission 014 playtest: The Gentle Guide

About 15–20 minutes, ideally with the Kamvas 13. The pen has only been simulated here.

1. Download **DoodleNook-win-x64** from the newest green run on
   [this branch](https://github.com/crosenlund79/DoodleNook/actions/workflows/windows-build.yml?query=branch%3Aiteration%2F014-the-gentle-guide).
2. Extract it and start `DoodleNook.exe`. The title says **DoodleNook — M014**.

## A. The whole lesson

1. Draw a line or two in the picture that opens, so it has unsaved changes.
2. **Help › Guided Tutorials…** One lesson: **Your First Animation**. Press **Start**.
3. The welcome card: **Start**. In step 1, press **Create practice animation**. You're asked about your unsaved
   picture first: try **Cancel** (nothing changes, you stay on step 1), then do it again and choose **Don't save** or
   **Save**.
4. A 256 × 256 animation at 8 fps opens in Animation Studio. Follow the guide: draw a ball, add a frame, onion skin,
   draw the bounce over 6–8 frames, play it, save it, export it.
5. Watch for:
   - the soft orange outline on the control to use next;
   - the guide noticing each step by itself, and moving on shortly after;
   - the guide never sitting on top of the control it points at;
   - **Save** and **Export** always asking you where, as usual.
6. On the last card ("You made your first animation!"), try **Return to animation**: the guide closes and your ball
   is still there.

## B. Leaving the path

1. Start the lesson again and use **Back**, **Skip step** and **I've done this**. Each should do what it says.
2. Drag the guide by its title bar somewhere else. It should stay there.
3. Press **Tab** to hide the interface while a step points at something. The guide says "Can't see it?"; **Show me**
   brings it back. Also try it from another workspace (Alt+1).
4. Close the guide with **×** in the middle of a step. The animation, its frames and Ctrl+Z all carry on as normal.
5. While the guide is open, use your usual shortcuts (B, E, N, O, Enter, [ ], Ctrl+Z, Alt+1…). They should all
   work. Escape keeps its usual meaning: it doesn't close the guide.

## C. Sizes

Try the lesson maximised and in a small window. If you can, also try it on the Kamvas at 150 %. The guide should
stay on screen, readable, and clear of the controls it points at.

### Questions

1. Is the guide's tone right: short enough, friendly, never in the way?
2. Is the outline easy to notice without being distracting? Should it pulse less, or more?
3. Does it move on at the right moments? Was anything noticed too early or not at all?
4. Is the default corner for the guide good, or would you rather it start somewhere else?
5. Would you use more lessons like this? Which ones (pixel art, palettes, Game Edit Mode, reference cycles…)?
