# Mission 010 playtest: The Finishing Touches

About 30 minutes, best with the Kamvas 13 and its pen. Use a copy of a drawing you like (or a copy of a game's
graphics folder). Everything below is undoable with Ctrl+Z. Note anything fiddly, slow or surprising: these tools are
meant to feel like drawing, and your hands are the judge (the pen has only been simulated so far).

1. Download **DoodleNook-win-x64** from the newest green run on
   [this branch](https://github.com/crosenlund79/DoodleNook/actions/workflows/windows-build.yml?query=branch%3Aiteration%2F010-finishing-touches),
   extract it and start `DoodleNook.exe`. The title says **DoodleNook — M010**.

## A. New artistic tools

1. **Reshape an illustration** (Painting): open a character drawing, **Edit › Liquify…** (Ctrl+Shift+X). Push a cheek
   outward, nudge an eye, try Inflate and Twirl (hold the pen still), then Restore (the last button) over a part
   you overdid. Ctrl+Z takes back one stroke; Enter applies, Esc cancels. Then Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z: the whole
   Liquify session is one step.
2. **Smudge colours**: the new button under the eraser (finger icon), or **Shift+B**. Paint two colours side by side and
   drag across their edge. Try Strength low and high, and Finger painting.
3. **Clone a texture**: right-click the smudge button › **Clone** (or **Shift+S**). Alt+click a nice bit of texture,
   then paint over a mark somewhere else. The dashed ring shows where it copies from.
4. **Repair an imperfection**: **J**. Paint over a speck or a small gap: it fills in from around it. For texture,
   Alt+click a source first (the bar switches to *Source*).
5. **Transform selected artwork**: select a painted area, **Edit › Perspective**, drag the corners, Esc; again and
   Enter. Then **Edit › Warp**: drag the grid's points to bend it like a banner. (Ctrl+T's bar has Free, Perspective
   and Warp too.)
6. **On a game graphic**: File › Game Edit Mode…, open an image, refine it with Liquify and Smudge, Ctrl+S. The PNG
   in the game folder changes and the image is In progress (not Complete).
7. **On palette-locked artwork**: a picture locked to a palette (or a game image with the project palette): smudge,
   clone, liquify, warp. Everything should stay in the palette's colours, with soft edges still soft.

## B. The quick palette (the Alt+1 wheel)

Right-click, or the pen's button, on the canvas (in Pure Sketch it's the whole interface).

1. Draw with the pencil. Open the wheel and **drag the centre** to size the brush: short moves for small corrections,
   further for big changes (the real size is drawn while you drag). Try Shift for extra fine, and the mouse wheel.
2. Tap the **brush pill** under the wheel and choose another brush (try one of your own from the Brush Laboratory).
   The wheel stays open: size it, then draw.
3. Open the wheel again and switch back to the pencil from **Recent**: it comes back at the size you used it at.
4. Open it near the edges of the screen and at different zooms.

### Questions

1. Is brush sizing in the wheel easy now, and precise enough for small corrections? Comfortable with the pen?
2. Is choosing a brush in the wheel quick enough? Do you miss anything (favourites first, fewer brushes, bigger tiles)?
3. Does the wheel still feel simple and quick, or does the pill get in the way?
4. Does Smudge feel like moving paint? Strength right by default?
5. Is Liquify's Push natural? Too strong or too weak at the default strength? Any lag on your bigger pictures?
6. Clone and Repair: is Alt+click for the source obvious enough? Does Repair blend well on your textures?
7. Perspective and Warp: are the handles easy to grab with the pen?
8. Did any of this get in the way of ordinary drawing?
